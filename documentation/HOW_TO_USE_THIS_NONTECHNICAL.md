# How to Use This — A Guide for Everyone Else

*The question this document answers:* **I don't know anything about code
or GitHub — how do I actually use this?**

You talk to an assistant, the same way you'd talk to a capable colleague.
You describe what you want written, changed, or fixed, in your own words.
The assistant does the actual work — you never touch a technical tool
yourself, and your account is deliberately set up so nothing you do can
break the project, even by accident.

## Sharing an idea about how things should work

While you work, you'll notice things: *"we should always mention the
client's name in the subject line," "never send this without someone else
reading it first," "let's stop doing it the old way."* Just say so, the
moment you notice it.

Your assistant repeats the idea back in your own words to make sure it
understood, then turns it into a proposed rule — what this project calls
a **practice**. From there, one of three things happens, depending on how
big the idea is:

- **It's just about how you personally like to work** (a habit, not
  something anyone else needs to follow): it applies to you immediately.
  There's nobody else who needs to agree.
- **It's a rule for your whole team:** your assistant posts it somewhere
  your team's approvers — the people trusted to say yes to shared rules —
  will see it and can respond, much like commenting on a shared document.
  One of them has to agree before it applies to everyone; you'll be told
  once it does.
- **It's useful enough that it should apply to everyone using this
  system, anywhere:** it goes up for a public review, and someone
  independent has to accept it before it becomes real.

Nothing you suggest is ever adopted without a real yes from whoever it
actually affects — and nothing you say is ever quietly forgotten either.
An idea nobody has acted on yet stays on record, not lost.

## Personal, team, and universal — and moving between them

Think of it as three shelves:

- **Your own shelf** — habits and preferences that are just yours. Nobody
  else sees or is bound by what's on it.
- **Your team's shelf** — rules everyone on your team follows. You can be
  on more than one team, each with its own shelf.
- **The public shelf** — rules shared by everyone, everywhere, using this
  system.

A rule usually starts on your own shelf or your team's, and can move
later — say, something you did personally turns out to be something your
whole team wants too. Just say so; your assistant handles the move, and
it still needs the same kind of yes described above at its new home
before it's official there. A rule never moves by itself.

## What you can't do (on purpose)

Your access is deliberately limited so nothing you do can break the
project or publish something by accident — you can comment and propose,
but you can't push changes live or approve them yourself. This isn't
about trust in you personally; it just means a mistake, including the
assistant's, stops at a wall instead of reaching anyone else.
