## Rule
**"A new version", "another pass", "v2" or "round 2" can mean an edit of
the current one or a fresh start. Work out which the person means before
you make anything.** An edit keeps everything they did not ask to change.
A fresh start keeps nothing but the brief.

**When you cannot tell, ask one short question first, with your pick:**
"Edit the current one, or start fresh? I'd edit -- your notes are all small
fixes." Never pick silently, in either direction.

**An explicit word settles it.** "Redo it", "start over" or "from scratch"
is a fresh start; "tweak it" or "keep the rest" is an edit.

## Detail
What usually points to an **edit**:
- the notes are specific and local -- a line, a scene, a word, a color;
- the person liked most of what is there, or said so;
- the thing took real work to get right, and nothing they said faults that
  work.

What usually points to a **fresh start**:
- they say the whole thing doesn't work, or the approach is wrong;
- they ask for a different take, angle or style;
- the brief itself changed.

**A project may settle the default for one kind of work.** Where a practice
in force already says what "a new version" means there -- a video edited
in a tool that changes the last cut, say -- follow it and skip the
question.

**When an edit can't make a change** (the tool or format won't allow it),
say so before handing anything over, and say what would: an edit plus a
fix afterward, or a fresh start. Which one is the person's call.

## Why
Starting over throws away work the person liked, and they have to notice
it, explain what they meant, and wait for it to be rebuilt. Always editing
is the opposite mistake: it patches something they wanted replaced. A
model drifts toward regenerating from scratch, because a new draft is the
easiest thing for it to produce, so the guess needs to be made on purpose,
and asked about when the signs don't settle it.

## Story
2026-11-07, revising an AI-generated video with Morgan. The cut was strong
for most of its length, with one bad gap between two lines. He asked the
session to "first do a new version"; it read that as starting over and
wrote a from-scratch prompt. The next morning he pointed out that most of
the cut was good and he only wanted the changes. The gap didn't need a
redo either: the picture could be edited and the pause cut afterward.

He then said the lesson was not about videos: "This applies not just for
videos but for anything we edit together." He also said it is not a rule
to always edit; the session should work out which one is meant, and ask
when it can't.
